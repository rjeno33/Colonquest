Frequently Asked Questions

A colonoscopy is a test that allows a specially trained clinician to examine the lining of your large bowel using a thin flexible camera. The test helps diagnose bowel symptoms and screen for bowel disease.

Most patients having a colonoscopy in the UK will be offered a choice of pain relief options. Select an option below to find out more.

Entonox is a mixture of oxygen and nitrous oxide (laughing gas). You breathe it through a mouthpiece and control when you take it.

It can help reduce discomfort and anxiety during the procedure, but you remain awake and able to communicate with the endoscopy team.

Afterwards: Entonox leaves your body quickly, and most people can return to normal activities soon after the procedure.

Conscious sedation involves medication given through a small injection into a vein. It helps you feel relaxed and sleepy while reducing discomfort.

You are not usually fully asleep, and you can still respond to the endoscopy team if needed.

Important: If you have sedation, you will need someone to take you home and stay with you afterwards. You should not drive, operate machinery, drink alcohol, or make important decisions for the rest of the day.

Iron tablets should be stopped a week before your colonoscopy.

Other medications such as blood thinners or anticoagulants may need to be stopped depending on your procedure; you should discuss this with your referring physician or hospital.

When you arrive at the endoscopy unit, a member of the team will check your details, explain the procedure, and answer any questions and complete a consent form and discuss options of pain relief and sedation.

You will be required to change into some special gowns and underwear shorts and you may need a cannula inserting.

You will be taken to the procedure room where you will meet the clinician performing your test and the nursing team, you will have the opportunity to discuss your symptoms and any further questions before starting the test

After your colonoscopy, you will be monitored until you are ready to go home.

The colonoscopy itself usually takes around 20–45 minutes, but you should expect to be in the endoscopy unit longer due to preparation, consent, recovery and discharge.
